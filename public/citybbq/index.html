Why you’re reading this

Randy Heldman and the IT team have already walked through the platform and the hardware with us. The part that belongs to marketing is the part that matters most: what City Barbeque sounds like.

You have designed nearly everything a guest sees, smells, and tastes: the smokers out front, the hickory in the parking lot, the sides in the steam line, and the founder’s handmade tables. Music is the one part of the experience nobody at City Barbeque chose. It came with a vendor contract.

01 · Our read on the brand

Craft,
made accessible.

City Barbeque isn’t selling speed, convenience, or price. It’s selling the feeling that someone spent eighteen hours on your brisket because you deserve food made the right way, and then handed it to you in under ten minutes.

“

We deliver the flavors of America. Rather than adhering to a single regional style, we decided to make the best barbecue that we know how to make.

City Barbeque · Nation’s Restaurant News

Where it comes from

A Kansas farm kid, a competition team, and a converted donut shop.

1999

Rick Malir leaves a job selling John Deere tractors and joins the Barbeque Boys, an award-winning competition team. Together they open the first City Barbeque in Upper Arlington, Ohio.

Day 1

Construction runs late, so Rick runs catering out of his garage to keep the lights on. The tables he built by hand are still in that first restaurant, with plaques.

Now

About 80 restaurants across 10 states, every one corporate-owned. The company even bought back a franchised location to put it under its own standards.

The lineage shows in the building. The smokers sit outside as proof, not tucked away in the back of the house. That’s a brand that wants guests to see the craft. We think they should hear it too.

The brand in two columns

What City Barbeque is, and what it refuses to be.

It is

  • Craft without pretense

    Competition-grade BBQ for a family on a Tuesday night, not a destination for BBQ snobs on a Saturday.

  • Pan-American by design

    Memphis, Texas, Carolina, and Kansas City sauces on the same table. It’s a curated best-of, not a purist’s shrine.

  • Generous

    Big portions, held on purpose while competitors shrink theirs, and a culture built on paying people well.

  • Woven into the neighborhood

    “Serve and Create Happiness,” carried out through thousands of local fundraisers every year.

It is not

  • Reheated

    Every plate is smoked on-site and served fresh. Many competitors use commissaries or reheat.

  • A regional purist

    Not “Memphis only” and not a Texas brisket temple. It’s inclusive and exploratory.

  • A franchise

    Every restaurant is corporate-owned, so quality and culture are non-negotiable everywhere.

  • A costume

    No theme-park country kitsch. It’s a real restaurant with real smokers.

02 · Who walks in

Four guests.
Four different rooms.

One stock channel can’t serve all of these people. The same restaurant is a different room at 12:15 than at 3:30 or 6:45, and the music should know it.

01

Dinner · 5–8 PM

The weeknight family

A parent after work with kids in tow. They want real food fast: not a drive-thru, and not a 90-minute sit-down.

The room should
Feel warm and welcoming, with enough energy for the table but quiet enough to talk.
What ruins it
Chaos, volume, or anything a parent has to explain in the car.

02

Lunch rush · 12–2 PM

The lunch-break crew

Job-site crews, office teams, and remote workers on a 30–45 minute window. Many are regulars who order ahead in the app.

The room should
Keep momentum without stress, because this is the money daypart.
What ruins it
A room that drags, or one that feels frantic when they need to recharge.

03

Afternoon · 2–5 PM

The catering & fundraiser planner

Feeding 15 to 100 people for an office lunch, a church supper, or a school fundraiser. Many come back through the fundraising program.

The room should
Sound like the brand they’re about to put their name on, and tell them what’s possible.
What ruins it
A room that feels like any other chain. That’s the moment they’re deciding who to trust.

04

Weekends · All day

The BBQ enthusiast

They judge bark, smoke ring, and sauce balance, and order the Judges’ Sampler to compare. They become evangelists when the craft story holds up.

The room should
Carry the craft story all the way through, including the speakers.
What ruins it
Anything that feels generic. They notice everything.

03 · What it should sound like

The flavors of America, curated like the menu.

The menu puts Memphis next to Texas next to the Carolinas, so the playlist should too. That means pan-American roots music: blues, soul, country, southern rock, and Americana, chosen with the same care as the hickory.

I.

Pan-American

Memphis soul next to Texas country next to Carolina folk. No stock channel thinks this way.

II.

Warm & analog

Real instruments and real voices. Nothing overproduced, nothing from the club.

III.

Daypart-aware

The lunch rush and the 3 PM lull are different rooms, so they get different music.

IV.

Family-safe

Adult taste with kid-safe lyrics. Every song, and every word, is reviewed.

Should sound

Warm, never corporate Confident in craft Mid-tempo & conversational Familiar & accessible Generous & easygoing Southern & heartland nods

Should never sound

08 · The hardware standard

PlayerOne.

One device in every restaurant. Nothing for anyone to touch.

Live status display showing the channel, Wi-Fi, and USB at a glance.

Today, when a restaurant’s network drops, the room switches to Christmas music. In July.

PlayerOne stores the whole program on the device. If the internet goes out, the music keeps playing exactly as scheduled. Guests never notice, and our team sees the alert.

Solid-state, built for 24/7

A commercial player with no moving parts and no consumer hardware. It’s built to stay on as long as the smokers do.

Plays through outages

On-board storage caches the program, so a network outage never means silence in the room.

Uses the network you have

Wired Ethernet or Wi-Fi. No new cabling, no audio VLAN, and no infrastructure project.

Nothing for staff to touch

Button-free and tamper-proof. There are no in-store settings, so there are no help-desk tickets.

Managed from one dashboard

Control, monitoring, and updates for every player, all on one screen.

A five-minute swap

It ships pre-configured for its restaurant. The old box comes out and PlayerOne goes in, and your speakers stay put.

In the box

3.5mm AV out · Ethernet · HDMI · Optical out · 12V power · Wi-Fi antenna · Live status display

For every new build

We manufacture the audio systems too: factory-direct speakers and amps, design and install from our in-house engineers, and 8,500+ certified technicians nationwide. We join your new-build pipeline and work with your GC, so each opening sounds like City Barbeque from day one.
