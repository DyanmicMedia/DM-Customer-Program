01 · The problem

No shortcuts in the smoker. One in the speakers.

Someone at City Barbeque decided every part of the visit a guest can see, smell, and taste. Nobody decided what they hear. The room plays a stock category channel, the fast-casual equivalent of reheated brisket. It works. It just isn’t you.

02 · Our read on the brand

Craft,
made accessible.

City Barbeque isn’t selling speed, convenience, or price. It’s selling the feeling that someone spent eighteen hours on your brisket because you deserve food made the right way, and then handed it to you in under ten minutes.

“

We deliver the flavors of America. Rather than adhering to a single regional style, we decided to make the best barbecue that we know how to make.

City Barbeque · Nation’s Restaurant News

Where it comes from

A Kansas farm kid, a competition team, and a converted donut shop.

1999

Rick Malir leaves a job selling John Deere tractors and joins the Barbeque Boys, an award-winning competition team. Together they open the first City Barbeque in Upper Arlington, Ohio.

Day 1

Construction runs late, so Rick runs catering out of his garage to keep the lights on. The tables he built by hand are still in that first restaurant, with plaques.

Now

About 80 restaurants across 10 states, every one corporate-owned. The company even bought back a franchised location to put it under its own standards.

The lineage shows in the building. The smokers sit outside as proof, not tucked away in the back of the house. That’s a brand that wants guests to see the craft. We think they should hear it too.

03 · Who walks in

Four guests.
Four different rooms.

One stock channel can’t serve all of these people. The same restaurant is a different room at 12:15 than at 3:30 or 6:45, and the music should know it.

01

Dinner · 5–8 PM

The weeknight family

A parent after work with kids in tow. They want real food fast: not a drive-thru, and not a 90-minute sit-down.

The room should
Feel warm and welcoming, with enough energy for the table but quiet enough to talk.
What ruins it
Chaos, volume, or anything a parent has to explain in the car.

02

Lunch rush · 12–2 PM

The lunch-break crew

Job-site crews, office teams, and remote workers on a 30–45 minute window. Many are regulars who order ahead in the app.

The room should
Keep momentum without stress, because this is the money daypart.
What ruins it
A room that drags, or one that feels frantic when they need to recharge.

03

Afternoon · 2–5 PM

The catering & fundraiser planner

Feeding 15 to 100 people for an office lunch, a church supper, or a school fundraiser. Many come back through the fundraising program.

The room should
Sound like the brand they’re about to put their name on, and tell them what’s possible.
What ruins it
A room that feels like any other chain. That’s the moment they’re deciding who to trust.

04

Weekends · All day

The BBQ enthusiast

They judge bark, smoke ring, and sauce balance, and order the Judges’ Sampler to compare. They become evangelists when the craft story holds up.

The room should
Carry the craft story all the way through, including the speakers.
What ruins it
Anything that feels generic. They notice everything.

04 · What it should sound like

The flavors of America, curated like the menu.

City Barbeque smokes the flavors of America, not one region. The playlist should do the same: blues, soul, country, southern rock, and Americana, chosen with the same care as the hickory. A well-traveled BBQ fan’s playlist, not a one-note regional stereotype.

Texas country, next to Memphis soul, next to Chicago blues. Same idea as the sauces on every table.

I.

Regionally inclusive

Never Memphis-only or Texas-only. The brand isn’t a purist, so the playlist isn’t either.

II.

Warm & live

Analog warmth, live instruments, and real singers. Nothing glossy, nothing Auto-Tuned.

III.

Vocals forward

BBQ is social food. Road songs, working songs, love songs, and celebrations, never background texture.

IV.

Family-safe, not kiddie

Music adults actually want to hear that happens to be safe for kids. Every lyric is reviewed.

It should sound

  • ✓

    Rooted in American craft traditions

    Blues, soul, country, southern rock, roots rock, and gospel-tinged R&B.

  • ✓

    Mid-tempo and conversational

    95–115 BPM for most of the day: enough for a busy room, easy enough to settle in.

  • ✓

    Familiar, not overplayed

    Mostly current, with throwbacks for depth. Songs guests like, not songs they’re tired of.

  • ✓

    Confident and easygoing

    A place to exhale and enjoy, never urgent or anxious.

  • ✓

    Shaped to the daypart

    Momentum through the lunch rush, more space in the afternoon, warmth at dinner.

It should never sound like

  • ✕

    Top-40 pop or dance

    Generic and corporate. It undercuts the craft story.

  • ✕

    Theme-park country

    No “Cotton Eye Joe.” City Barbeque is a real restaurant, not a costume.

  • ✕

    Stadium country or bro-country

    Too slick and overproduced to feel craft-rooted.

  • ✕

    Spa, ambient, or soft-rock mush

    The sound of every other fast-casual chain.

  • ✕

    Aggressive rock or metal

    Families with young kids are a core guest. Nothing hostile.

07 · The hardware standard

PlayerOne.

One device in every restaurant. Nothing for anyone to touch.

Today, when a restaurant’s network drops, the room switches to Christmas music. In July.

PlayerOne stores the whole program on the device. If the internet goes out, the music keeps playing exactly as scheduled. Guests never notice, and our team sees the alert.

Solid-state, built for 24/7

No moving parts and no consumer hardware. Built to stay on as long as the smokers do.

Plays through outages

On-board storage caches the program, so an outage never means silence.

Uses the network you have

Wired Ethernet or Wi-Fi. No new cabling, no audio VLAN.

Nothing for staff to touch

No in-store settings, so no help-desk tickets.

Managed from one dashboard

Control, monitoring, and updates for every player on one screen.

A five-minute swap

Ships pre-configured. The old box comes out, PlayerOne goes in, and your speakers stay put.

In the box 3.5mm AV out · Ethernet · HDMI · Optical out · 12V power · Wi-Fi antenna · Front display

New builds We also design, manufacture, and install the speakers and amps, with 8,500+ certified technicians, so each opening sounds like City Barbeque from day one.

09 · Phase two, optional · In-store messaging

Get the music right. Then test the voice.

The room your guests hear for the entire visit is the one channel City Barbeque doesn’t use. Messaging isn’t part of the core program. Once the music is live, we’d run a short, measured test to see whether a few well-made spots move the numbers you already track.

If it moves the needle, it rolls out. If it doesn’t, we turn it off, and the music stays exactly as it is.

  1. 01

    Pick a handful of spots

    Four to six fleet-wide spots, written with your team and voiced by professional talent. The same spots play in every test restaurant.

  2. 02

    Run them for 45 days

    A small test group hears the spots. Similar restaurants stay music-only, as the control.

  3. 03

    Read the numbers, then decide

    Compare test against control. Roll it out, refine it, or turn it off.
